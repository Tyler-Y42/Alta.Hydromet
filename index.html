                Methods and QC

                Which stations, and why

                • Gauges along the canyon. Seven precipitation gauges from the ridge to the canyon mouth: Alta Collins (CLN, 9,662 ft, the Alta avalanche program's storm plot, hourly), Albion Basin (PC064, PacifiCorp), Alta Guard House (AGD), Alta RWIS (UTALC, UDOT), White Pine (UTWLC, UDOT), Elberts (ELBUT), and Powerhouse (UTPLC, 6,049 ft, near the mouth). Brighton (PC056) and Spruces (SPC) are over the ridge in Big Cottonwood and are shown for comparison only.
                • How the gauges compare: from 9 August to 8 October 2026, PC064 and AGD caught 144.3 and 144.0 mm, the other canyon gauges 127 to 136 mm, and Spruces 140 mm. CLN caught 94 mm and was missing about 19% of its hourly reports, so treat CLN as a timing check rather than a total.
                • Alta base area (ATB) recorded 0 mm in both the 10/7 and 10/8/2026 storms while its neighbors tipped. It is shown, flagged in Table 2 whenever it reads zero while the other Alta gauges catch 1 mm or more, and left out of storm detection. Hidden Peak (HDP) is used for ridge temperature only, and the Atwater study plot (ATH20) for snow depth.
                • Snowpack: SNOTEL Atwater (1308, in Alta since 2022), Snowbird (766, record since 1989, with a median), Brighton (366, Big Cottonwood). Daily values only; hourly SNOTEL precipitation has 0.1 in steps and thermal noise.
                • Streamflow: Salt Lake County Flood Control gauge 220 at Tanners Flat (7,117 ft) is the only active gauge in the canyon, above the Salt Lake City and Metropolitan Water District diversions. The old canyon-mouth gauge (USGS 10167500, CBRFC LCTU1) closed in 2008. Valley gauges are in the supplement.
                • Radar: the NEXRAD national mosaic (IEM, 5 min). It shows convective cells that fall between gauges, as on 10/8/2026 when a hail-producing cell sat over Albion Basin from about 12:25 to 1:45 PM while PC064 recorded 0.76 mm.

                Precipitation QC

                • PacifiCorp and UDOT gauges: sum of 10 min accumulations. PacifiCorp's since-midnight field resets near 01:00 MDT and is never differenced. CLN: sum of hourly accumulations.
                • AGD, ELBUT, SPC: first differences of cumulative precip_accum. Negative steps (bucket dumps, resets) count as 0. A step that spans more than 30 min is attributed to the later report and flagged.
                • Increments faster than 10 mm per 10 min are flagged suspect and left out of totals unless another gauge recorded precipitation within 30 min.
                • Tip size 0.254 mm, so every total is ±0.25 mm. A value stamped t covers the interval ending at t. "No data" (no reports) is distinct from 0. A total is partial when its last report is more than 25 min before the end of the interval. Every load re-fetches at least the last 72 h.
                • Tipping buckets undercatch snow and hail, and unheated buckets hold snow until it melts. Totals in cold or convective storms are minimums. Table 1 flags gauges that caught nothing below 1 °C and storms seen by one gauge only.

                Radar estimate

                • Mosaic reflectivity (dBZ) is read from IEM map tiles at about 120 m pixels. Rain rate uses the Marshall Palmer relation Z = 200 R1.6 with a 53 dBZ cap for hail. Echoes below 30 dBZ count as 0: on the dry days of 10/4 and 10/5/2026 terrain clutter and other non-weather echoes reached 23 to 33 dBZ over the watershed and added 0.2 to 1 mm a day of false rain at lower floors. The cost is that light snow, often 15 to 30 dBZ, does not show in the radar line; the gauges cover it.
                • The estimate is uncalibrated. The radars see the canyon from well above the ground, terrain blocks part of the beam, and hail inflates reflectivity. Use it for timing and location, and as a check on the gauges, not as a total.
                • A radar cell is any period with watershed maximum reflectivity of 35 dBZ or more for at least 10 min. Cells outside gauge storms are listed in Table 1 as R1, R2, ….
                • The GitHub Action reads one mosaic tile per 5 min frame every 15 min and stores the series on the data branch, so the page loads a few kilobytes instead of hundreds of tiles; only frames newer than the last run are decoded in the browser. Stored radar starts 8 September 2026.

                Freezing level

                • Each hour, a straight line is fit to air temperature against elevation for every station reporting (6,049 to 11,000 ft); the freezing level is where that line crosses 0 °C. It is left blank when the fit is poor (r² < 0.6) or temperature rises with height (an inversion). Above about 12,000 ft it is an extrapolation.

                Storms and response

                • Tips from the detection gauges (CLN, PC064, AGD, UTALC by default) are pooled in time order; a gap over 3 h starts a new group. A group is a storm when any detection gauge totals at least 1.0 mm.
                • Rain or snow: precipitation weighted mean of each gauge's own air temperature; below 1 °C is called mixed or snow.
                • Tanners Flat response: baseline is the time weighted mean of the 6 h before the start; peak is the maximum up to 48 h after the end, cut at the next storm. Lag runs to the peak from the centroid of the rain that fell before it, at every gauge above Tanners Flat (CLN, PC064, AGD, UTALC, UTWLC, ELBUT); storms with two bursts would otherwise place the centroid after the first peak. A rise of two gauge steps (about 0.3 cfs) or less is no clear response. A peak before the centroid gets no lag, since the rise came from earlier precipitation.
                • Flow QC: a report far from the median of its five neighbors on each side is removed (this survives small clusters such as the 64 to 235 cfs readings of 10/8/2026 evening) (Tanners Flat stage dropped from 0.36 to 0.04 ft for one report, twice on 10/8/2026). A wild newest report is held until the next one confirms it.

                Snow depth QC

                • Values outside 0 to 4,000 mm are dropped. UTWLC depth is never used (it reads 719 mm in October). A nonzero depth that does not change at all for 48 h is a stuck sensor and is hidden (CLN read 1,371.6 mm on every report in October 2026). A sonic sensor over bare ground reads grass and rocks, so any reading of 150 mm or less within 3 h of a 0 is shown as 0.
