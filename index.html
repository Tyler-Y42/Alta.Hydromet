Methods and QC

Precipitation QC
  • PacifiCorp (PC064 Albion Basin, PC056 Brighton): sum of precip_accum_ten_minute. Their precip_accum_since_local_midnight resets near 01:00 MDT (midnight standard time) and is never differenced.
  • Avalanche network gauges (AGD, ELBUT, SPC): first differences of cumulative precip_accum. Negative steps are bucket dumps or resets and count as 0 (flagged reset). A step spanning more than 30 min is attributed to the later report (flagged gap).
  • Suspect jumps: any increment faster than 10 mm per 10 min is flagged. It is shown, but left out of totals unless another gauge recorded precipitation within 30 min of it.
  • Resolution: 0.254 mm (0.01 in) per tip, so every total is ±0.25 mm. Totals print to 0.01 mm. Sums are rounded to 0.001 mm to strip floating point noise.
  • Intervals: a 10 min value stamped t covers (t − 10 min, t]. A window total includes every report with a time stamp inside (start, end].
  • Late data: each station shows its last report. "No data" means no precip report in the interval, which is different from 0.00 mm. A total is marked partial when the last report is more than 25 min before the end of the interval. Every load re-fetches at least the last 72 h because values arrive late and get revised.
  • UTALC (Alta RWIS): its precip_accum_ten_minute and present weather text are shown for context only. It is a road weather sensor, not a gauge, so it never enters storm detection or corroboration.
  • Undercatch: unshielded tipping buckets undercatch snow, often badly. Treat totals during snow as minimums.
Snow depth QC
  • Values outside 0 to 4000 mm are dropped. UTWLC is never used (it reads an impossible 719 mm in October).
  • A sonic sensor over bare ground returns grass and rocks: ATH20 flips between 0 and 75 to 127 mm hour to hour in early October. Any reading ≤ 150 mm within 3 h of a reading of 0 is treated as bare-ground noise and shown as 0.
  • Single spikes more than 100 mm off the 5 report running median are replaced by the median.
Storm detection and streamflow response
  • Included tips from the detection gauges (default PC064 and AGD) are pooled in time order. A gap longer than the max gap (default 3 h) between consecutive tips starts a new group. A group is a storm when any detection gauge totals at least the threshold (default 1.0 mm) in it.
  • Start is the beginning of the first tip's interval, end is the last tip's report time. Station totals cover (start, end]. Peak intensity is the largest single 10 min increment.
  • Rain or snow: the precipitation weighted mean of each station's own air temperature (nearest report within 20 min of each tip). Below 1 °C is called mixed or snow.
  • Centroid: precipitation weighted mean time of the detection gauges' tips.
  • Baseline Q: time weighted mean (each report held until the next) over the 6 h before the start. Peak Q: maximum from the start to 48 h after the end, cut short at the next storm's start or at the end of the record (flagged). Lag: centroid to the first time the peak is reached.
  • Flow QC: a single report far from the median of its three neighbours on each side (more than half the median and more than 5 resolution steps) is removed and marked on the chart. Tanners Flat stage dropped from 0.36 to 0.04 ft for one report, twice on 10/8/2026. The newest report has no later neighbour, so a wild newest value is held out until the next report confirms it.
  • A peak that comes before the storm centroid gets no lag: the rise came from earlier precipitation (for example a cell that missed the detection gauges). A flow already above baseline at the storm start is flagged too.
  • A rise of no more than two gauge resolution steps (the smallest change between distinct reported values, about 0.3 cfs at Tanners Flat) is reported as no clear response. In spring, daily snowmelt cycles can be larger than a storm response; check the chart before trusting a lag.
Streamflow sources
  • LCC @ Tanners Flat (county gauge 220, 7117 ft) is in the canyon above the Salt Lake City and Metropolitan Water District diversions, so it is the closest available record of the upper canyon's response. Discharge is computed from stage at 0.01 ft resolution, so it moves in steps of about 0.3 cfs at low flow.
  • Canyon mouth: CBRFC point LCTU1 (the former USGS 10167500 site) closed in 2008 and has no observations. No public canyon-mouth record for LCC is available; the CSV slot above is for one.
  • BCC @ Canyon Mouth (county gauge 320) is below the Big Cottonwood water treatment intakes, so low flows reflect diversions.
  • LCC @ Crestwood Park, LCC @ 300 West, and USGS 10168000 (LCC at the Jordan River) are valley gauges below the diversions and the urban storm drain network. Their storm response reflects valley runoff and operations, not the upper canyon.
  • County data cannot be read cross origin from a browser, so a GitHub Action copies it every 15 min into this site's data branch. GitHub delays scheduled runs, so expect 15 to 45 min of lag. USGS values are provisional.
